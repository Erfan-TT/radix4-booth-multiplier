\begin{frame}{Project scope and result}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{Goal}\par
Close the gap between a hand-built signed multiplier and the one Design
Compiler builds from \texttt{A * B} with its DesignWare library, in latency,
area and power, under the same library and constraints.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{Approach}\par
Refine a radix-4 Booth multiplier in structural VHDL, one step at a time:
behavioural Booth $\rightarrow$ Wallace tree $\rightarrow$ sign-extension
elimination $\rightarrow$ Dadda tree $\rightarrow$ fused selector.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{Measurement}\par
Every version is synthesised over a clock sweep, placed and routed, simulated
with its routed delays, and measured for power.
\end{column}

\begin{frame}{Radix-4 Booth recoding}

Overlapping triplets of the multiplier operand select one signed digit
$d_i\in\{0,\pm1,\pm2\}$:

\[
A B = \sum_{i=0}^{N/2-1} d_i A\,4^i,
\qquad B_{-1}=0
\]

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccl@{}}
\toprule
triplet & $d_i$ & generated value \\
\midrule
000, 111 & $0$  & $0$ \\
001, 010 & $+1$ & $+A$ \\
011      & $+2$ & $+2A$ \\
100      & $-2$ & $-2A$ \\
101, 110 & $-1$ & $-A$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
\small
The recoder reduces the partial-product count from $N$ to $N/2$. At $N=32$,
the datapath reduces 16 Booth rows plus one correction row.

\vspace{7pt}
The shift for $2A$ changes wiring positions. Conditional negation adds logic
to every row and becomes an important optimization target.
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Step 1: Wallace tree}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{From an adder chain to a tree}\par
The behavioural start sums the Booth rows with a chain of additions. The
Wallace version compresses them with rows of full adders, three rows into
two, until two rows remain for one carry-propagate adder.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{The trade}\par
A tree's depth grows with the logarithm of the row count, so it targets
latency. It pays for this in full adders and wiring.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
 & chain & Wallace \\
\midrule
fastest $T_0$, synth. & 2.00\,ns & \textbf{1.86\,ns} \\
fastest $T_0$, layout & \textbf{1.74\,ns} & 1.76\,ns \\
area at fastest point & \textbf{7986\umarea} & 10022\umarea \\
energy/op, layout & \textbf{14.9\,pJ} & 27.0\,pJ \\
real wire delay & \textbf{0.16\,ns} & 0.65\,ns \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{8pt}
\small
$7\%$ faster in synthesis, but larger, about $80\%$ more energy after layout,
and no faster once wired. Steps 2--4 keep the tree and remove its cost.
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Step 2: Sign-extension elimination}

Each Booth value fits in $N+1$ two's-complement bits. Explicit sign extension
adds $N^2/4=256$ replicated bits to the $32$-bit partial-product array.

\vspace{4pt}
For sign bit $s$, the identity $-s=\bar{s}-1$ gives

\[
-s2^N=\bar{s}2^N-2^N.
\]

The row keeps its lower $N$ bits, stores the inverted sign bit, and fills the
upper positions with zero. Row $i$ then has a fixed positive bias
$2^{N+2i}$, independent of the operands.

\[
C=-\sum_{i=0}^{N/2-1}2^{N+2i}\pmod{2^{2N}}
=\mathtt{0xAAAAAAAB}\ll N \quad (N=32)
\]

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.57\textwidth}
\small
The correction constant occupies the upper half of the existing Booth
increment row. It needs no additional reduction-tree row.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.39\textwidth}
\small
Array dots: $800$ to $561$\par
Minimum area: $6346$ to $5975\umarea$
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Step 3: Generated Dadda reduction}

The Wallace tree instantiates full-width carry-save adders. The Dadda
architecture instead places compressors only where a column exceeds the next
target height.

\[
d_0=2,\qquad d_{j+1}=\left\lfloor\tfrac{3}{2}d_j\right\rfloor
\qquad 2,3,4,6,9,13,19,\ldots
\]

At $N=32$, the elaboration-time schedule uses six stages with targets
$13,9,6,4,3,2$.

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
\small
The scheduler must include carries arriving from column $k-1$:

\[
h_{\text{next}}=C_{\text{in}}+n-2F-H\leq d_{\text{target}}.
\]

It records full-adder sources, half-adder sources and pass-through dots in
three matrices. Generate statements use those matrices for exact port maps.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
Wallace RTL instances & 960 FA \\
Dadda schedule & 435 FA, 45 HA \\
\addlinespace
Minimum area & $5975$ to $5620\umarea$ \\
Fastest $T_0$ & $1.87$ to $1.73$\,ns \\
\end{tabular}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Step 4: Fused Booth selector}

The original row generator first selected $A$, $2A$ or zero, then applied an
explicit conditional inversion to every bit. The revised architecture exposes
selection and negation as one Boolean function per bit.

\[
q_i=c_{+1}a_i+c_{-1}\bar a_i+c_{+2}(2a)_i+c_{-2}\overline{(2a)_i}.
\]

The four controls are mutually exclusive. A zero row sets all four controls to
zero. Design Compiler can therefore map the combined cone without preserving a
separate XOR boundary.

\vspace{5pt}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
at a 3.0\,ns constraint & XOR/XNOR & \texttt{FA\_X1} & cells & area \\
\midrule
Dadda, separate negation & 1230 & 117 & 3936 & 5725\umarea \\
\textbf{Dadda, fused selector} & \textbf{85} & \textbf{448} & \textbf{2819} & \textbf{5125\umarea} \\
Inferred \texttt{A * B} & 559 & 268 & 3035 & 4910\umarea \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{5pt}
\raggedright\footnotesize
The paired shift in XOR and adder cells supports improved adder recognition,
while the total cell count falls by $28.4\%$.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Takeaways}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{The gap is almost closed}\par
After layout, the final version is within $1\%$ of the DesignWare
multiplier's latency, $13\%$ of its area and $10\%$ of its energy. It is
better than the behavioural start on all three.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{The steps are unequal}\par
The Wallace tree bought latency only in synthesis, at a high energy cost.
Sign-extension elimination gave $6\%$ area. Dadda gave area and speed. The
fused selector gave the largest single step: $-10\%$ area and $-48\%$ energy.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{RTL boundaries steer the tool}\par
Removing the XOR layer let the mapper use compound cells and full adders. The
gain came from what the tool could recognise, not from fewer operators.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{Confirm after layout}\par
The wire-load model charged every version about $1.0$\,ns, but the real wires
cost $0.16$--$0.67$\,ns. Rankings made in synthesis should be checked after
place and route.
\end{column}
\end{columns}
