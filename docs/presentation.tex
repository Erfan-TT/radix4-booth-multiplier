\begin{frame}{Project scope and result}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{Engineering question}\par
How close can a hand-built signed multiplier come to the implementation that
Design Compiler infers from \texttt{A * B} under the same library and
constraints?

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{Implementation}\par
A configurable structural VHDL datapath with a radix-4 Booth front end, a
Wallace or Dadda reduction tree, and a sparse carry-propagate adder.

\vspace{8pt}
\textbf{Three changes}\par
Remove explicit sign extension, generate a Dadda schedule at elaboration, then
merge Booth selection with conditional negation.
\end{column}

\begin{frame}{Radix-4 Booth recoding}

Overlapping triplets of the multiplier operand select one signed digit
$d_i\in\{0,\pm1,\pm2\}$:

\[
A B = \sum_{i=0}^{N/2-1} d_i A\,4^i,
\qquad B_{-1}=0
\]

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}ccl@{}}
\toprule
triplet & $d_i$ & generated value \\
\midrule
000, 111 & $0$  & $0$ \\
001, 010 & $+1$ & $+A$ \\
011      & $+2$ & $+2A$ \\
100      & $-2$ & $-2A$ \\
101, 110 & $-1$ & $-A$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.50\textwidth}
\small
The recoder reduces the partial-product count from $N$ to $N/2$. At $N=32$,
the datapath reduces 16 Booth rows plus one correction row.

\vspace{7pt}
The shift for $2A$ changes wiring positions. Conditional negation adds logic
to every row and becomes an important optimization target.
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

% =============================================================================
\begin{frame}{Sign-extension elimination}

Each Booth value fits in $N+1$ two's-complement bits. Explicit sign extension
adds $N^2/4=256$ replicated bits to the $32$-bit partial-product array.

\vspace{4pt}
For sign bit $s$, the identity $-s=\bar{s}-1$ gives

\[
-s2^N=\bar{s}2^N-2^N.
\]

The row keeps its lower $N$ bits, stores the inverted sign bit, and fills the
upper positions with zero. Row $i$ then has a fixed positive bias
$2^{N+2i}$, independent of the operands.

\[
C=-\sum_{i=0}^{N/2-1}2^{N+2i}\pmod{2^{2N}}
=\mathtt{0xAAAAAAAB}\ll N \quad (N=32)
\]

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.57\textwidth}
\small
The correction constant occupies the upper half of the existing Booth
increment row. It needs no additional reduction-tree row.
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.39\textwidth}
\small
Array dots: $800$ to $561$\par
Minimum area: $6346$ to $5975\umarea$
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Generated Dadda reduction}

The Wallace baseline instantiates full-width carry-save adders. The Dadda
architecture instead places compressors only where a column exceeds the next
target height.

\[
d_0=2,\qquad d_{j+1}=\left\lfloor\tfrac{3}{2}d_j\right\rfloor
\qquad 2,3,4,6,9,13,19,\ldots
\]

At $N=32$, the elaboration-time schedule uses six stages with targets
$13,9,6,4,3,2$.

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.54\textwidth}
\small
The scheduler must include carries arriving from column $k-1$:

\[
h_{\text{next}}=C_{\text{in}}+n-2F-H\leq d_{\text{target}}.
\]

It records full-adder sources, half-adder sources and pass-through dots in
three matrices. Generate statements use those matrices for exact port maps.
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.42\textwidth}
\scriptsize
\begin{tabular}{@{}lr@{}}
Wallace RTL instances & 960 FA \\
Dadda schedule & 435 FA, 45 HA \\
\addlinespace
Minimum area & $5975$ to $5620\umarea$ \\
Fastest $T_0$ & $1.87$ to $1.78$\,ns \\
\end{tabular}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Fused Booth selector}

The original row generator first selected $A$, $2A$ or zero, then applied an
explicit conditional inversion to every bit. The revised architecture exposes
selection and negation as one Boolean function per bit.

\[
q_i=c_{+1}a_i+c_{-1}\bar a_i+c_{+2}(2a)_i+c_{-2}\overline{(2a)_i}.
\]

The four controls are mutually exclusive. A zero row sets all four controls to
zero. Design Compiler can therefore map the combined cone without preserving a
separate XOR boundary.

\vspace{5pt}
\centering\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrr@{}}
\toprule
at a 3.0\,ns constraint & XOR/XNOR & \texttt{FA\_X1} & cells & area \\
\midrule
Dadda, separate negation & 1230 & 117 & 3936 & 5725\umarea \\
\textbf{Dadda, fused selector} & \textbf{85} & \textbf{448} & \textbf{2819} & \textbf{5125\umarea} \\
Inferred \texttt{A * B} & 559 & 268 & 3035 & 4910\umarea \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}

\vspace{5pt}
\raggedright\footnotesize
The paired shift in XOR and adder cells supports improved adder recognition.
Cell counts cover the whole netlist, so they do not prove one-to-one mapping of
RTL compressors.

\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Conclusions and remaining work}

\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.55\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{Main findings}

\vspace{3pt}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep5pt
\item Sign-extension elimination removes $29.9\%$ of the array dots, but only
      $5.8\%$ of the minimum area because synthesis already removes many
      constant paths.
\item The generated Dadda schedule gives the largest timing improvement.
\item The fused selector gives the largest area improvement by changing how the
      reduction logic maps, not merely by deleting explicit XOR operators.
\end{itemize}
\end{column}

\begin{column}{0.40\textwidth}
\small
\textbf{What the project demonstrates}

\vspace{3pt}
RTL structure affects both direct gate cost and the patterns a synthesis tool
can recognise.

\vspace{10pt}
\textbf{Next evidence needed}

\vspace{3pt}
Formal equivalence and SDF simulation would strengthen correctness evidence.
Place-and-route and activity-based power would show whether the pre-layout
advantages survive physical implementation.
\end{column}
